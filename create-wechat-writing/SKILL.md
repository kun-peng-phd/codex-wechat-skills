# Create WeChat Writing

Write a publishable Chinese research article, or revise an existing one without changing its defensible meaning. Treat a user-supplied article as the primary voice reference; use [voice-profile.md](references/voice-profile.md) when the user asks for the Ag Commodity Research style or for language like the supplied August 2026 WASDE article.

## Establish the article contract

- Identify the intended industry reader, central question, requested length, publication date, source vintage, and any words or claims the user has excluded. Infer these from the brief when clear.
- State one qualified main judgment before arranging sections. Keep the piece organized around why the data matter, not the order of a report's tables.
- For revisions, preserve the original numbers, causal logic, caveats, links, and figure insertion points unless the user asks to change them or verification reveals an error. Flag material factual corrections.

## Build the evidence chain

- Prefer original statistical releases and dated primary sources. Compare the same marketing year, geography, unit, and statistical category across periods.
- In each section, move from **judgment → numerical evidence → mechanism → boundary**. Show a balance-sheet bridge when a stock change has several causes; do not count a component twice or mistake a residual category for direct consumption.
- Separate official estimates, observed conditions, forecasts, survey expectations, and author inference. Mark the cutoff date for weather, crop progress, prices, and positions. Do not describe pre-release data as a post-release response.
- When discussing China, explain the path through origin quotes, quality, shipment window, freight, policy, and demand. Do not mechanically convert a foreign stock change into a Chinese landed-price change.
- Do not invent social consensus or quote unverifiable X, LinkedIn, or Substack posts. If public evidence is sparse, say so in source notes rather than filling the gap with unattributed sentiment.

## Write for the phone screen

- Open with the contrast or decision that matters, then give readers the main conclusion in one compact paragraph.
- Use short, answer-oriented section headings. Give each crop, sector, or causal mechanism its own judgment when their directions differ.
- Keep paragraphs purposeful, usually one claim and its support. Use selective bold for the conclusion, not entire paragraphs. Explain what each chart proves at its insertion point.
- Close with concrete questions or data releases to follow, not a generic prediction. Respect the user's requested length and banned terms; do not impose this article's preferences on unrelated writing.

## Release check

Read the piece aloud for stiff translations, forced metaphors, duplicated conclusions, and unnecessary rhetorical questions. Recheck figures, dates, units, forecast labels, source links, image captions, and the stated data cutoff. When images or a distribution package are requested, hand the final text to `$create-wechat-data-cards` and `$create-wechat-publish-package`; neither handoff authorizes publishing or sending.
